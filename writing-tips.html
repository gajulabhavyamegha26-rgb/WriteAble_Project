Writing Tips

Clear writing is easier to read, easier to understand and easier to check. These tips work for emails, assignments and reports.

1. Plan before you write

Decide who will read your writing and what you want them to know or do. Then list your three main points. A short plan saves a lot of rewriting.

2. Put the main point first

Tell the reader the most important thing in the first sentence or two. Add the details after that.

3. Keep sentences short

Aim for about 15 to 20 words in most sentences. If a sentence has more than one idea, split it.

Instead of: Because the meeting that was scheduled for Monday has been moved, you should note that it will now take place on Tuesday.

Write: The Monday meeting has moved to Tuesday.

4. Prefer the active voice

In an active sentence, the person or thing doing the action comes first. It is usually shorter and clearer.

Instead of: The report was written by the team.

Write: The team wrote the report.

5. Use plain words

  • Use "use" instead of "utilize".
  • Use "begin" or "start" instead of "commence".
  • Use "to" instead of "in order to".
  • Use "because" instead of "due to the fact that".

6. One idea per paragraph

Start each paragraph with its main idea, then support it with details or examples. Start a new paragraph when the idea changes.

7. Read it aloud

Mistakes and awkward sentences are easier to hear than to see. Use the Read aloud option in the Accessibility button, or the microphone on the Editor page to speak your first draft.

8. Proofread in passes

  1. First check that the content is complete and in a sensible order.
  2. Then check grammar and spelling.
  3. Finally check names, dates and numbers.

If you can, take a short break before you proofread. You will notice more.

9. Use the checker, but think for yourself

The Check Writing button in the Editor finds many mistakes, but its suggestions are not always right. Read each suggestion and decide whether it fits what you mean.
