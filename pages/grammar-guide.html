Grammar Guide

A quick reference for the mistakes people make most often. Each rule has a short example.

Words that are often mixed up

Commonly confused words, their meanings and examples
Words Rule Example
its / it's "Its" shows belonging. "It's" means "it is" or "it has". The dog wagged its tail. It's a friendly dog.
their / there / they're "Their" shows belonging. "There" is a place, or is used in "there is". "They're" means "they are". They're putting their bags over there.
your / you're "Your" shows belonging. "You're" means "you are". You're sure this is your book?
then / than "Then" is about time or order. "Than" is used to compare. Finish the work, then rest. This is easier than that.
affect / effect "Affect" is usually a verb (to influence). "Effect" is usually a noun (a result). Lack of sleep can affect your mood. The effect is easy to see.

Subject-verb agreement

The verb must match the real subject of the sentence, not the word nearest to it.

Incorrect: The list of items are long.

Correct: The list of items is long.

Run-on sentences and comma splices

Two complete sentences cannot be joined with only a comma. Use a full stop, a semicolon, or a joining word such as "and", "but" or "so".

Incorrect: I finished the essay, I submitted it.

Correct: I finished the essay, and I submitted it.

Also correct: I finished the essay. I submitted it.

Sentence fragments

A sentence needs a subject and a complete idea. A group of words that starts with "because" or "although" needs a main clause to go with it.

Incorrect: Because it was raining.

Correct: We stayed home because it was raining.

Apostrophes

  • Use an apostrophe to show belonging: the student's book (one student), the students' books (many students).
  • Do not use an apostrophe to make a plural: two apples, not two apple's.
  • Use an apostrophe for a missing letter: don't, can't, it's.

Keep the tense consistent

Do not switch between past and present without a reason.

Incorrect: I opened the file and then I save it.

Correct: I opened the file and then I saved it.

Capital letters

Use a capital letter at the start of a sentence, for names of people and places, and for the word "I".
